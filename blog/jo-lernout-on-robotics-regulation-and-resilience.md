# Jo Lernout on Robotics, Regulation, and Resilience

![Jo Lernout and Unitree Go2](/jo/jo.jpg)

This week’s Tech\&Meet was a memorable one, not just for the technology on display, but for the speaker behind it. We had the honor of welcoming **Jo Lernout**, a name that still resonates in Belgium's tech history. Known for his trailblazing work in voice recognition, and later for one of the most dramatic rises and falls in European tech, Jo brought experience, insight, and some showmanship to our campus.

---

## 🧓 The Man Behind the Mic: A Look at Jo Lernout’s Legacy

Jo Lernout is best known as the co-founder of **Lernout & Hauspie**, the pioneering speech recognition company that took Belgium and much of the tech world by storm in the 1990s. The company was a darling of the NASDAQ, with clients including Microsoft and Intel, and was considered the future of voice tech.

But things unraveled quickly.

In 2000, Lernout & Hauspie collapsed in what would become one of Belgium's largest corporate fraud scandals. A series of accounting irregularities, especially in its Asian operations, led to bankruptcy. Jo, along with co-founder Pol Hauspie, faced legal battles and convictions for fraud. He served time but has since resurfaced in the tech world, speaking and consulting, often with candid reflections on his journey.

Far from hiding his past, Jo speaks openly about it, making him a uniquely grounded and seasoned voice in an industry that often moves too fast for reflection.

---

## 🤖 Present and Future: Robots in a Regulated World

Jo’s talk focused on the challenges and opportunities of **robotics in Europe**, particularly within the tightly controlled regulatory frameworks of the EU.

> “Europe wants to lead in ethics—but it risks being led by others in innovation.”
> — _Jo Lernout_

He touched on key themes:

- **How EU regulation slows deployment** of robotics compared to other regions.
- **Stories from his own ventures** attempting to navigate those rules.
- **Opportunities in healthcare, logistics, and elder care**, where robots could play a transformative role.

The talk felt less like a TED-style pitch and more like an honest fireside chat, pragmatic, sometimes philosophical, and occasionally provocative.

---

## 🐾 Meet Unitree Go2: His Four-Legged Friend

What stole the show, however, was Jo’s robotic sidekick: the **Unitree Go2**, a quadruped robot that looked like it had trotted straight out of a sci-fi film.

Equipped with:

- **4D LiDAR**
- **GPT-based AI interaction**
- **Crazy strong torque motors**
- **Autonomous navigation**

…the Go2 showed off a range of movements and behaviors that left everyone in awe. It walked, jumped, climbed stairs, and responded to voice commands. Watching this sleek, dog-like machine interact with students was both inspiring and slightly surreal.

---

## 🤕 The Curious Case of Stephen Lernout

Jo was scheduled to be joined by his son, **Stephen Lernout**, but he was "unable to attend due to an accident.” Jo briefly mentioned it at the start of the session, but curiously, some students noticed that Stephen was listed as a speaker at a tech conference in another country the very next day.

Was it a scheduling mix-up? A convenient exit? Who knows, but it added a touch of mystery to an otherwise fully transparent session.

---

## 🍷 Wrapping It All Up

After the talk and Go2 demo, we had a casual wrap-up with snacks, drinks, and great conversations. Jo stayed for a while, answering questions and mingling with students. The atmosphere was light-hearted, despite the heavy topics and a reminder of why these Tech\&Meet sessions matter so much.

---

## 🎯 Final Thoughts

Jo Lernout’s visit was a rare combination of deep tech insight, industry experience, and personal narrative. From the early internet boom to robot dogs, his journey is a microcosm of modern tech: fast-moving, unpredictable, and always evolving.

While the past may cast a long shadow, Jo’s presence at our university was a clear sign that reinvention is possible and that the best lessons often come wrapped in both success and failure.
